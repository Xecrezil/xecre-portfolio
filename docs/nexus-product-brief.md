## Step 0 — Capstone Preparation (Systems Analysis & Design)

### Objective

Prepare students for the capstone journey by forming project groups, conceptualizing research ideas, and producing initial title proposals before enrolling in Capstone Project 1.

---

### Stakeholders

- Students
- Faculty Members / Prospective Advisers
- Program Chair

---

### Inputs

- Capstone guidelines
- Academic calendar
- Grouping policies
- Existing capstone documentation (limited)
- Available business partners
- Department requirements (e.g., AI or IoT integration)

---

### Activities

- Students form project groups.
- Initial research ideas are discussed.
- Project titles are proposed.
- Business domains are explored.
- Preliminary feasibility analysis is conducted.
- Groups prepare documentation for title evaluation.

---

### Outputs

- Finalized project groups
- Initial title proposals
- Preliminary documentation
- Candidate research topics

---

### Pain Points

#### Institutional Knowledge

- Previous capstone documentation was difficult to access.
- Students had little visibility into completed projects.

#### Proposal Transparency

- Ongoing proposals from other groups were not visible.
- Previously rejected titles were undocumented.
- Duplicate ideas were often discovered late.

#### Team Formation

- Students were grouped under institutional constraints rather than complementary skills and commitment.

#### Adviser Selection

- Adviser expertise and availability were difficult to identify.

#### Planning

- AI and IoT requirements introduced uncertainty.
- Technology stack decisions lacked guidance.
- Business data gathering was constrained by time.

---

### Opportunities

- Searchable institutional repository
- Proposal discovery and similarity analysis
- Adviser expertise profiles
- Project planning templates
- Requirement knowledge base
- Team formation support
- Timeline and milestone tracking

---

### Notes

Within the BSIT curriculum, Systems Analysis and Design effectively functioned as an unofficial "Capstone Project 0," where the foundations of every capstone project were established long before Capstone Project 1 officially began.

## Step 1 — Proposal Submission & Title Evaluation

### Objective

- Validate the feasibility and relevance of proposed capstone projects.
- Evaluate project scope and academic quality.
- Identify duplicate or substantially similar proposals.
- Approve project titles before development begins.
- Facilitate adviser assignment based on faculty availability and expertise.

---

### Stakeholders

- Students (individuals before final group formation)
- Subject Instructor
- Program Chair
- Faculty Evaluators
- Prospective Research Advisers
- Proposal Defense Panel

---

### Inputs

- Proposed project title
- Project rationale
- Chapter 1
- System proposal
- Presentation slides
- Client or business information
- Feasibility study

---

### Activities

1. Students prepare proposal documents.
2. Proposal defenses are conducted in place of traditional midterm and final examinations.
3. Faculty evaluators review the proposal and presentation.
4. Feedback and recommendations are provided.
5. Students revise their proposal when necessary.
6. Titles are approved or rejected.
7. Students secure a research adviser after title approval.

---

### Outputs

- Approved project title
- Revised proposal documentation
- Assigned research adviser
- Rejected proposal (requiring revision or replacement)
- Proposal defense results

---

### Pain Points

#### Proposal Visibility

- Students had limited visibility into previously proposed or approved titles.
- Duplicate or substantially similar projects were often discovered late in the process.

#### Documentation

- Proposal status and evaluation records were maintained through fragmented or manual methods.
- Students lacked a centralized record of review history and decisions.

#### Adviser Assignment

- Adviser selection depended on faculty availability, expertise, and capacity.
- Students often competed for limited adviser slots.

#### Communication

- Feedback and announcements were distributed across multiple communication platforms.
- Important updates could easily be overlooked.

#### Technical Guidance

- Newly introduced AI and IoT requirements created uncertainty for both students and faculty.
- Guidance on selecting appropriate technologies varied depending on adviser experience.

---

### Opportunities

- Proposal repository with searchable history
- Proposal similarity and duplication analysis
- Centralized review and feedback management
- Proposal status tracking
- Adviser expertise directory
- Requirement and technology guidance
- Unified communication and notification system

---

### Notes

- Proposal defenses replaced conventional midterm and final examinations.
- Collaboration and project management tools such as GitHub, Figma, and Notion were primarily self-taught rather than formally integrated into the curriculum.
- Adviser availability was constrained by faculty workload and departmental policies.
